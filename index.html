02 / Professional philosophy

Physics-aware models. Software-grade execution.

I bridge complex aerospace dynamics and modern software engineering by turning operational reality into explicit computational structure: nodes, edges, trajectories, constraints, receptor impacts, and measurable decision criteria. The goal is not merely to produce a mathematically valid path. It is to produce a route whose safety assumptions, efficiency tradeoffs, and regulatory dependencies can be inspected and defended.

That demands fidelity at two scales. At the system level, routing must reflect vehicle performance, airspace procedures, weather, mission energy, and the communities beneath a corridor. At the execution level, deterministic rule evaluation, microsecond-sensitive scoring primitives, efficient geospatial queries, and reproducible data pipelines determine whether an algorithm can move from a research notebook into an operational architecture.

Safety before novelty Tradeoffs made visible Latency measured Decisions traceable

Validated doctoral research 2026

Noise-Optimized Routes for Air Taxi

Developed a coupled vertiport-siting, route-network, trajectory-refinement, and acoustic-evaluation framework for Advanced Air Mobility. The model compares baseline, horizontal, altitude, and integrated routing candidates under efficiency, balanced, noise, and sensitive-receptor priorities.

Result: noise-priority solutions reduced class-balanced Lmax,95 by up to approximately 3.9 dB and SEL95/EPNL95 by approximately 6 dB, while preserving origin-destination structure at a modest route-length cost.

Four Methods for Noise-Aware Route Optimization

The dissertation evaluates route geometry, altitude, competing operational objectives, and schedule effects as connected parts of one planning framework rather than isolated analyses.

Together, the methods expose where acoustic benefit comes from, what operational penalty it introduces, and how route-level improvements change under efficiency, noise, sensitive-receptor, and time-of-day priorities.
